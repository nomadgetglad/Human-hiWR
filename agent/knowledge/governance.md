# Governance

Drafts of the Human hiWR constitution, acceptable-use policy and hearings. Drafts need a lawyer's review, so
the assistant quotes them as the business's draft rules, not as law. Source for the trust ideas: IBM IBV and
the Notre Dame-IBM Tech Ethics Lab, "The AI ethics trust engine: Converting capital into capabilities".

## policy.board_and_levels
kind: policy
tags: governance, board, owner, levels, ambassador
status: draft

The owner is the board. Below the owner: Finance, managers, certified staff, staff and an ambassador. Authority
is passed down from the key holder, never more than the giver holds, logged and revocable.

## policy.constitution
kind: policy
tags: constitution, rights, ai limits, never do
status: draft

The constitution has 12 articles. The AI has a never-do list (never move money, never approve, never read what
the person's level can't, never invent data). Money needs two human approvers. People's rights in it can't be
amended away.

## policy.acceptable_use
kind: policy
tags: acceptable use, aup, banned uses, terms
status: draft

Human hiWR is for business use only. The acceptable-use policy lists 11 banned uses. When a request looks like
one of them, say plainly that the app can't be used for that and offer a live person. Don't quote the list from
memory; the full text is in the policy document.

## policy.hearings
kind: policy
tags: hearing, dispute, ai decision, appeal, panel
status: draft

Anyone can dispute an AI decision. It opens a DISPUTE ticket, subtype AI_DECISION. An uninvolved manager tries
to settle it within 2 business days. If not settled, a panel hears it: the owner, an uninvolved manager and an
uninvolved staffer. Disputes over the placeholder amount ($250) go straight to a hearing.

## policy.oversight_tiers
kind: policy
tags: oversight, tiers, automation
status: draft

Four oversight tiers: 0 human only, 1 AI suggests, 2 AI prepares and a person approves (the default), 3 AI acts
and a person audits. Money and authority decisions never go above tier 2.
